- The number of employed people increased by 63,000 year over year.

- June imports reached USD 66.1 billion, up 30.0% year over year.
- The June trade surplus was USD 36.1 billion.

SECTION 4: REPORTED MARKET CONTEXT

- WTI rose as US-Iran and broader Middle East tensions increased concern about oil supply.
- Lower June US CPI temporarily reduced expectations for an immediate Fed rate increase.
- South Korean semiconductor shares sold off as investors questioned the sustainability of AI capital spending and memory-price growth.
- On July 8, the KOSPI entered bear-market territory after falling more than 20% from its late-June record close.
- On July 16, the KOSPI fell 6.37% as technology shares declined, Middle East risk increased, and the Bank of Korea raised rates.
- Strong current semiconductor earnings did not prevent share-price declines because markets focused on future earnings growth and valuation.
- The Bank of Korea cited inflation, won volatility, housing prices, household debt, and financial stability as reasons for raising rates.

SECTION 5: CROSS-MARKET CHANNELS TO EVALUATE

- US cloud and AI capital spending affects Korean HBM and memory demand.
- US semiconductor weakness can be amplified in the KOSPI because Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have large index weights.
- Higher oil prices can raise inflation and import costs in energy-dependent South Korea.
- Higher US yields and a stronger dollar can pressure the won and foreign investment flows.
- A Bank of Korea rate increase may support the won but can pressure equity valuations and leveraged positions.
